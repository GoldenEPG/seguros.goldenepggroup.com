Quick AnswerReviewed August 15, 2026

What does “liability coverage” mean?

Liability coverage is the portion of an insurance policy that may respond to certain covered claims when the insured becomes legally responsible for injury, property damage, negligence or another covered loss. The amount and scope of protection depend on the policy’s limits, exclusions, conditions and endorsements.

Policy Limits

Three liability limits business owners often see.

Different policies use different limit structures. These are common concepts, not guarantees of coverage.

Each Occurrence / Per Accident

The maximum amount the policy may pay for one covered occurrence or accident, subject to policy terms.

General Aggregate

The maximum amount available for certain covered claims during the policy period under applicable policy provisions.

Products / Completed Operations Aggregate

A separate aggregate may apply to certain covered claims involving products or completed work.

Coverage Basics

Limits are only one part of liability coverage.

Two policies can show the same limits while providing different actual protection because policy language, exclusions and endorsements can differ.

Covered Causes of Loss

The policy defines what kinds of events or allegations may qualify for coverage.

Exclusions

Exclusions identify situations, activities or types of loss the policy does not cover.

Conditions

Policy conditions may define duties, reporting requirements and other obligations.

Endorsements

Endorsements can add, remove or modify policy provisions, subject to carrier approval.

Coverage Boundaries

Common exclusions can be just as important as the limit.

A high limit does not mean every type of claim is covered. Policy exclusions should be reviewed alongside the declarations and endorsements.

Auto Exposure

Vehicle liability generally requires appropriate auto coverage rather than relying on a standard General Liability policy.

Employee Injuries

General Liability is not intended to replace workers’ compensation or employers liability coverage.

Professional Services

Advice, errors, omissions or professional services may require separate Professional Liability coverage.

Contracts & Requirements

Requested limits do not automatically change the policy.

A landlord, customer, general contractor or project owner may ask for specific liability limits or endorsements. Those requirements must be matched against the actual insurance policy.

Requested Limits

Contracts may specify limits such as per occurrence or aggregate amounts. Availability is subject to underwriting.

Additional Insured

Additional insured status must be supported by the policy and applicable endorsement.

Waiver Requests

Waiver of subrogation or other requirements must be supported by applicable policy provisions or endorsements.

Certificate of Insurance

A COI shows evidence of coverage — it does not create coverage.

The actual policy and endorsements determine coverage. A certificate cannot expand or modify the insurance contract.

Evidence of Insurance

A COI can help show that insurance is in force at the time it is issued.

Policy Controls

If the certificate and policy differ, the policy terms and endorsements control coverage.

Texas Requirements

For Texas risks, certificate forms and use should comply with applicable Texas requirements.

Texas regulatory reference

Texas Department of Insurance provides public guidance on Certificates of Insurance. These references do not imply TDI endorsement of Golden EPG Group.

Liability Coverage FAQ

Common questions about limits and coverage

What is liability coverage?

Liability coverage is the protection provided by a liability insurance policy for certain covered claims where the insured is legally responsible for injury, property damage, negligence or another covered loss.

What is an each occurrence limit?

An each occurrence limit is generally the maximum amount available for one covered occurrence, subject to the policy terms and applicable exclusions.

What is an aggregate limit?

An aggregate limit is generally the maximum amount available for certain covered claims during the policy period, subject to the specific policy.

Does a COI guarantee coverage?

No. A Certificate of Insurance is evidence of insurance and does not create, expand or change coverage beyond the actual policy and endorsements.

Can a contract require higher liability limits?

A contract may request specific insurance limits or endorsements, but those requirements must be matched against what the insurer actually agrees to provide.

Are exclusions important even when limits are high?

Yes. A high policy limit does not mean every claim is covered. Exclusions, conditions and endorsements can materially affect the scope of coverage.
